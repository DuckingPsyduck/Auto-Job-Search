# Code of Conduct

## The short version

People come to this project in the middle of a job search. That is often a rough stretch —
rejection, money pressure, a layoff someone hasn't told their family about yet. Treat everyone
here with that in mind.

Be decent. Assume the other person is doing their best with what they know. Criticize the code,
not the person who wrote it.

## What's expected

- **Be direct and be kind.** These are not in tension. "This will break on Workday because the
  dates are relative" is both.
- **Assume good faith**, especially across a language barrier or a first contribution. Not
  everyone here writes English as a first language or has contributed to a project before.
- **Accept that the answer is sometimes no.** A maintainer declining a change is not a personal
  judgment, and neither is a contributor declining to do more work.
- **Respect people's privacy.** Don't ask for someone's CV, employer list, real name or search
  history to debug an issue, and don't post anyone's personal data — including your own.

## What's not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments, in issues, pull requests or anywhere else the
  project happens.
- Discrimination or unwelcome attention based on age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or
  expression, level of experience, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, sexual
  identity or orientation, immigration or visa status, employment status, or military service.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, reopening settled decisions to wear people down,
  or demanding unpaid work from volunteers.
- Using this project to build something that deceives job seekers or employers — fabricated
  experience, mass automated applications, or anything that submits on a person's behalf without
  their knowledge. That is out of bounds technically and socially.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code and
comments — and anywhere someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

If someone's behavior is a problem, report it privately through GitHub — **Report content** on
the offending comment, issue or pull request, or the **Report a vulnerability** form under this
repository's Security tab for anything that exposes personal data. Both reach the maintainers
without the report being public.

Please don't open a public issue about someone's conduct. It escalates the thing you are trying
to stop, and it puts whoever was affected in the middle of it.

Reports are read by the maintainers, kept confidential, and answered. You will not be asked to
confront the person yourself, and reporting in good faith will never be held against you — it
does not matter whose pull request is open.

## What happens next

Maintainers will look into it and decide what's warranted. Depending on what happened, that
ranges from a private word, to asking for a public apology, to a temporary or permanent ban from
the project. Maintainers who break these rules face the same consequences, more so — holding the
merge button is not a defense.

We will explain a decision to the people directly involved. We will not litigate it in public at
the expense of whoever was affected.
